Understanding Oral Cancer

What is Oral Cancer?

Oral cancer develops in the tissues of the mouth or throat, including the lips, tongue, cheeks, gums, floor of the mouth, and roof of the mouth.

In many cases, oral cancer is diagnosed at later stages, reducing treatment success rates significantly.

AI-assisted screening tools can help identify suspicious lesions, tissue irregularities, and abnormal patterns much earlier.

Warning Signs

Common Symptoms

Persistent oral abnormalities should never be ignored.

Persistent Mouth Sores

Sores that do not heal within two weeks.

Red or White Patches

Unusual patches inside the mouth or on the tongue.

Difficulty Swallowing

Pain or discomfort while chewing or swallowing.

Risk Factors

Common Causes

Lifestyle and environmental factors contribute heavily to oral cancer development.

Tobacco

Smoking and chewing tobacco significantly increase risk.

Alcohol

Excessive alcohol use increases oral tissue damage.

HPV Infection

Certain HPV strains are linked to oral cancers.

UV Exposure

Excessive sun exposure may cause lip cancer.
